habit shredding

carve a life worth living.

sometimes it feels like i don’t control my life at all.

i say i’m quitting, i do it again.

i say i will, but i won’t.

‎

habits are tricky like that.

you know what i mean?

‎

the problem?

they usually run in the background.

subconscious. automatic. hard to catch.

‎

so… are we just slaves to them?

some people are, some aren’t.

‎

the difference?

the ones on autopilot don’t know about the mountain.

‎

‎

‎

the mountain

to understand habits, you have to understand the subconscious.

to understand the subconscious — you gotta meet the mountain.

‎

think of a huge mountain, buried in snow.

this is your mind.

‎

now imagine a snowboarder shredding down the peak.

this is you doing something.

‎

every time you do something, a 133% sicker version of you shreds down the mountain and leaves behind a path.

‎

what you did and how you did it determines the shape of this path.

‎

like forming a habit — one ride doesn't leave a deep path.

go down often enough though — and the path carves itself.

‎

in fact, a path can get so deep that it becomes hard not to follow it.

crossing a deep path like this often leads to getting stuck in it — instead of just a partial overlap.

‎

"every time i act, i'm carving a new path — or deepening an old one.

now what?"‎

‎

‎

‎

finding your path

building a new habit? you're on fresh snow.

no path to follow. too many to avoid.

‎

lose focus — and you'll easily fall back into default mode.

‎

so how do you know which path is the one?

‎

habits live in different areas of the mountain.

on day one, you're not following — you're locating, visiting, and carving a new route.

‎

your first path is gonna be ass — you're still learning.

your next runs will be cleaner — deepening a trail that’s easy to follow.

until it's so deep — the snow remembers for you.

‎

the mountain is hard to navigate. everything's white.

grown man in sandals, tube socks & zero shame type of white.

‎

hard doesn’t mean impossible though.

with experience we start to remember more details.

‎

you recognize landmarks near each "habit area" and use these as waypoints.

‎

"if you see the big pine, you know you're close to area one."

‎

these waypoints aren’t just how you find your way.

they eventually do the work for you.

‎

‎

‎

the funnel effect

every time you pass the pine tree and hit area one, the path gets deeper.

after a while — the pine becomes a funnel to that area.

‎

get close — and you'll feel it.

unless you actively steer away, you get sucked in.

the deeper the path, the easier to follow — harder to avoid.

‎

same goes for funnels.

‎

if a funnel doesn't serve you,

it's a big pain in the ass to deal with.

‎

it's pushing you from every angle —

straight to the habit area.

‎

this is why you open up socials on your phone before even realizing it.

every single path around your phone funnels to doomscrolling if you're not careful.

‎

not only did you carve that path a ridiculous amount of times.

each carve did more damage than usual.

‎

you’ll see why once we bring emotion into the mix.‎

‎

‎

‎

emotional weight

we’re emotional beings, so it only makes sense that emotion plays a big role here.

emotions amplify any path we carve — good or bad.

‎

‎the more emotional the run — the heavier the rider.

every path gets deepened way faster.

‎

normally it takes a few runs to get a clean path.

now it only takes one.

‎

a few rejections — and someone makes a habit of self-doubt.

one trauma — and life changes forever.

‎

the same applies to dopamine

or anything else that completely captures your attention.

‎

every action carries more weight in the neural path creation.

loops are created without any effort.

‎

scroll for memes

→ dopamine release

→ sick version of you becomes heavy

→ leaves deeper path

→ gets stuck in it more

→ repeat

‎

now that we met the mountain —

let's rip some peaks.

‎

‎‎

‎

how to shred

let's check all our gear before we hit the slopes.

quick recap — remind you what we're working with:

‎

  • your subconscious is a snowy mountain
  • every action = path carved
  • repetition & emotion = deepening paths
  • deep path = easy to follow, hard to avoid
  • landmarks (waypoints) are how you navigate
  • path cross-overs at waypoints act as funnels
    ‎
    how do we adjust?
    ‎

path escape

if you ruined your mountain — it doesn't mean you are.

the snowboarder only stays stuck in paths when you’re not conscious.

stay awake — go anywhere you want.

‎

i said escaping was "hard" —

but in reality, that only means more discomfort.

you just have to put up with more mental bitching.

‎

with clarity you realize every path is emotional.

you are always free to override them.

‎

‎

‎

changing course

whether you want to change old habits or create new ones — same thing.

lock in discipline early. use emotion. use waypoints.

‎

actually, use waypoints that haven’t been used before.

if you wanna take up reading — do it in a spot you never sit in.

this way every trail leading to that chair? leads to books instead of screens.

‎

claim a corner of the mountain.

train your brain to link "this spot" to "this path".

‎

add to it.

create funnels.

let your environment do the work for you.

‎

‎completely changing your environment is really powerful too.

all this fresh snow — unaffected by the old you.

‎

even if you already have paths somewhere — just stay conscious.

‎‎

cross them.

paint over them.

be consistent.

‎‎

before you know it you're a whole new person.

‎

trying to remove old paths doesn’t work.

all you can do is paint new ones over them.

‎

that's why people fail when trying to abstain from vices without adding virtues.

‎‎

‎

‎

expert level

this last part is only for the truly passionate riders.

the ones that wanna optimize their subconscious.

‎‎

when learning a skill — you’re essentially carving a new path too.

works the exact same way.

‎

you wanna make it as easy as possible to perform well —

to follow the right path.

‎

so when you learn a new skill:

‎

iterate.

add variation.

start from the wrong position.

‎

this makes the new path wider — more robust, easier to follow.

‎

if you only score from a certain angle, you can struggle to recreate that angle every time.

or you just learn to score from multiple angles — and actually learn what it means to aim.

‎

habits, skills, memories, personality, identity, they all work the same.

‎

the most effective way to change is using repetition & emotion.

the most robust change comes from wide paths and smart waypoints.

‎

now go carve something worth shredding.

‎a 133% sicker version of your life is waiting.‎
